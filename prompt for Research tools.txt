OUR IDEA
In sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with only 3, 6 and 9 input photos,
methods like InstantSplat use an AI foundation model (MASt3R / DUSt3R / VGGT) to
predict (1) camera poses and (2) a dense 3D point cloud, and then (3) refine the
camera poses during training. We plan an ablation study that separates these
three parts by changing one at a time:
- perfect (reference) poses vs AI-predicted poses, in the same pipeline
- AI-predicted 3D points vs random points
- pose refinement on vs off
We test this on the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset at 3, 6 and 9 views, measuring PSNR,
SSIM, LPIPS, camera pose error (ATE/RPE) and training time.
